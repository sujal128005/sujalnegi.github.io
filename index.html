Winner · IBM SkillsBuild South Region Finale 2026

Limen

AI purchasing infrastructure where the spending limit is enforced, not trusted.

A multi-agent tender marketplace that compresses a two-to-three-month procurement cycle into days. Buyer ceilings and seller floors live in a Solidity contract, so no agent can raise its own spending limit.

  • Sealed bidding, escrow settlement and reputation scoring
  • Raise, approve and pay split across three role-gated desks
  • Every figure comes from a deterministic engine, verified by 539 automated checks

About

Engineer by training, builder by instinct.

I’m a mechanical engineer who works across manufacturing, software and program management. I design vehicle hardware, ship live AI systems and lead a 19-member autonomous vehicle team.

On the hardware side that means chassis, steering and drive design for a ground vehicle, and mass and component placement for a drone. On the software side it means agentic marketplaces and real-time verification tools that people can open and use today.

When I take on a problem I own all of it: the design, the team, the deadline and the result.

How I operate

01

Spend only after the proof

On our autonomous drone, hardware spend waited for a milestone in simulation. Cheap proof first, expensive parts second.

02

Separate who asks from who pays

Limen splits raise, approve and pay across three role-gated desks: the control a good purchasing team uses, built into software.

03

Let the model talk, let the rules decide

In Limen and Khata the AI writes the words and a deterministic engine owns the facts, backed by 863 automated checks.

04

Build toward a customer

The autonomous vehicle runs with a risk register and a go-to-market plan, not just a demo. Limen was pitched to IBM judges and won.
